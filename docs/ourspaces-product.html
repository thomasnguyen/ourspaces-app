Where the idea comes from

2005. A page you made,
with your top eight on it.

People used to own a corner of the internet that looked like them — bad fonts, autoplaying song, friends ranked in public. Then everything became a feed somebody else arranges.

We wanted that back, except shared, and except alive.

The 2005 feeling03

What it is

A space is one board
your group shares.

You drop things onto it. Everyone sees them, in the same place, at the same moment — and they're still there tomorrow.

  • You put things on it — a poll, a countdown, a photo, a question, a list of who's bringing what.
  • Everyone else sees it happen, live, with no refresh and no notification to tap.
  • It accumulates. A space three months old looks like three months of a friendship.

Why it feels real08

The front door

Every space has
its own email address.

Not an inbox you check. You send something to the space, and it becomes an object on the wall.

forward an email→a thing appears on the board→everyone sees it
  • Mail the crew a receipt and it turns into a row on the expense sheet.
  • Mail us two anything and it lands as a sealed letter you click to unfold.
  • Mail the build room a link and it drops into the pile with its cover and summary already filled in.

The beat10

The half nobody expects

And it writes back.

The space isn't a database you put things into. It answers, in the same email thread, in its own voice.

  • Straight away — it tells you what it did with what you sent, and names the file if you attached one.
  • Every Friday — it emails around what the week looked like, to everyone who's ever written to it.
  • Writing to a space is how you subscribe to it. There's no settings page for this.
Email out11

Getting in

You just walk in.

Open the link and you're already someone — you get a name, a colour and a cursor before you've typed anything. You can vote, draw and claim a slot straight away.

  • No form, no password, no wall in front of the board.
  • If you want to keep it, you type your email and get a six-digit code back. That's the whole sign-up.
  • Joining keeps everything you already did — your votes, your name, your colour. You're upgraded, not restarted.
No signup wall12

Who this is actually for

Groups that already exist.

We're not asking anyone to start a community or grow an audience. The people are already together in a chat — this gives them somewhere to put things down.

The five or six friends

planning a birthday, splitting a cabin, keeping the same joke alive for four years.

The two people

in different time zones who want somewhere shared that isn't a message thread.
