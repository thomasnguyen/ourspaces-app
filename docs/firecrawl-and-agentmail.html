Firecrawl · 1 of 5

01Scrape

A link becomes a card.

What you see

Paste a link into the build room. A grey placeholder appears, then fills in with a real card — title, description, cover image.

convex/firecrawl.ts · scrapeLink
  • Firecrawl does the extraction, not us. We ask for four formats at once — markdown, summary, images, and a JSON format with a prompt. We never parse HTML ourselves.
  • Hacker News links resolve properly. An HN link points at a comments page. We ask the HN API what story it is, scrape the article, and keep the points and comment count.
  • Cached 1 hour, retried with backoff. A re-shared link costs zero credits. A network blip doesn't lose the save.
  • A card is never blank. Title falls back through four sources, description through five.

Firecrawl · 2 of 5

02Search

A topic becomes a pile of cards.

What you see

In the build room, type a topic instead of a URL. The room fills with things to read.

convex/firecrawl.ts · searchTopic
  • A genuinely different surface from scrape. Search-then-clean in one call — not "search somewhere else, then scrape each result."
  • Results come back shaped exactly like the link cards the pile already renders, so there's no second card design.
  • The result limit is clamped to 1–20, because it's caller-supplied and Firecrawl bills per credit.

Firecrawl · 3 of 5 · the showpiece

03Crawl

A whole site, streaming in live.

What you see

The crawl strip. Pages appear as Firecrawl finds them — a live counter, a growing list, each page keepable into the pile.

crawlSite · CrawlStrip.tsx
  • Pages land in Firecrawl's own Convex tables, and the UI subscribes to them.
  • We never copy pages into a widget, never poll, never write refresh logic.
  • A second person watching the same space sees the same pages arrive at the same moment.

That's the demo nobody else has: a crawl with two people watching it.

Firecrawl · 4 of 5 · new

04Parse

An emailed document becomes text.

What you see

Email a receipt PDF to a space. The body can say nothing but "see attached" — the amount still lands on the right expense tracker.

convex/firecrawl.ts · parseDocument
  • No file ever touches our storage. AgentMail hands back a short-lived download URL, and /scrape parses a document URL identically to /parse.
  • So this is a scrape with a PDF parser attached — same call we already make for link cards, pointed at a different URL.
  • Guards, because parsing costs credits: inline logos skipped, 10 MB cap, documents only, 3 per email.
  • Every failure is soft. An unreadable PDF routes on the email body exactly as before. An attachment must never cost us the email.

Firecrawl · 5 of 5

05Stale-link refresh

Cards that don't rot.

What you see

Nothing — which is the point. Old cards quietly stay accurate.

convex/batch.ts · Fridays 17:00 UTC
  • Link cards older than 7 days get re-scraped, 5 at a time.
  • A hundred stale links don't become a hundred simultaneous Firecrawl calls — the cron enqueues, a batch worker drains.
  • Interrupted mid-run? It resumes from a cursor.

01Inboxes

Every space has a real address.

SpaceAddressWhat mail becomes

  • So creation is idempotent and internal-only. It used to be a public wrapper that let any caller make one. That was closed deliberately.

AgentMail · 2 of 7

02Webhooks

Mail arriving, safely.

  • Signature-verified. Standard-webhooks/svix HMAC — we sign id.timestamp.body and compare. An unsigned POST gets a 401.
  • Deduped. AgentMail can redeliver. We record the event id, so an email is never filed to the canvas twice.
  • The component owns the store. Dedup and the inbound table live behind one boundary, not scattered through app code.
convex/http.ts · /api/agentmail/webhook

AgentMail · 3 of 7

03Send

The space writes back.

What you see

Friday 16:00 UTC, every mail-enabled space emails its week to everyone who has ever emailed it. Mailing a space subscribes you to it.

  • The digest is a durable workflow: recipients → canvas snapshot → AI-composed lines → send.
  • Each step retries independently, and the whole run survives a server restart. A plain action would start over.
  • Rate-limited to 10/hour per space so a retry loop can't burn the free tier.
convex/digest.ts · sendEmail

AgentMail · 4 & 5 of 7

04Reply & Label

It answers you, in the same thread.

What you see

Email the crew a receipt. You get back:
“Logged $242 from Jules for tahoe cabin on the expense tracker. Read tahoe-cabin-receipt.pdf.”

  • Naming the file back to you is the tell that the space actually opened it.
  • The router's verdict also becomes a label on the message — receipt, booking, letter, links, spam, filed, otp.
  • So AgentMail's own console shows what our app decided, without us building a dashboard.
  • Deliberately best-effort. If the reply fails we swallow it — the email was already filed, and a mail hiccup must not undo a committed write.

06Attachments

Documents are readable.

attachment→AgentMail download_url→Firecrawl parse→the router reads it
  • The robustness bit. If the webhook carries attachments, we use them. If the key is absent entirely, we re-fetch the message rather than assume there were none.
  • An empty array means the email genuinely had none — and costs no extra call. That distinction is the whole fallback.

AgentMail13

AgentMail · 7 of 7 · the one people don't expect

07It signs you in

There is no separate email provider in this app. When you join OurSpaces, the six-digit code is sent through AgentMail.

  • No new credential. The integration the spaces already use does one more real job.
  • It must not create a fourth inbox. All three slots are spoken for, so sign-in rides the account-level inbox on purpose.
  • Six digits from the CSPRNG, 20-minute expiry — the code's lifetime and the email copy share one setting.
  • Rate-limited on the destination address, not the caller. Anonymous sign-in is free, so an attacker controls their own id — the thing worth protecting is the victim's inbox.
  • Guests upgrade, they don't fork. Join and the email is patched onto the same row — votes, name, colour, memberships all survive.
  • A requested code is a claim, not a fact. Nobody can type a stranger's address, never read the code, and still weld it onto their account.

Worth saying out loud

What we chose
not to build.

“We didn't get to it” and “we decided against it” sound very different.

  • Firecrawl Map — would make the crawl strip less of a black box: show the URL list, then crawl. Cheap. Honest answer: we ran out of demo, not ability.
  • Firecrawl Interact / Agent — genuinely out of scope. Nothing in a shared canvas wants to fill in a form on someone else's website.
  • AgentMail Drafts — the best remaining idea. We already frame ambiguity as an interaction (“a sealed envelope a human opens and files”). A draft reply a human approves is that idea one step further.
  • AgentMail WebSockets — webhooks are correct here. A Convex HTTP action is already push-driven; a socket adds a connection to babysit for nothing.
  • Custom domains — @agentmail.to in the UI is a feature at a hackathon. People recognise it.
