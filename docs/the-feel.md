# OurSpaces — the feel

A digital bedroom wall. A shared scrapbook. Not a dashboard.

---

The space should feel like something people left stuff on — stickers half-peeled, a photo taped crooked, a note someone meant to take down. Intentionally loose. Lived-in. A little chaotic on purpose.

Consistency doesn’t come from alignment grids. It comes from the *materials*: the paper, the candy light, the sticker chrome, the way type shouts on one object and whispers on another. Same family of things, different skins.

Color is soft until it isn’t. Pastel blooms in the air. Then a black dock. Then a neon plus. Then someone else’s cursor in their own loud color. The contrast is the point.

Typography is confident and a little rude. Big lowercase titles. Heavy. Occasional stamped labels — LIVE, 3 DAYS — like something pressed onto the page.

Objects don’t all look the same. A poster next to a cutout next to a sticky note next to a dark chat brick. You should be able to tell what something *is* before you read it.

When things move (later), they should feel like you picked them up. Slight lift. Tiny tilt. A shadow that means weight. Not bounce. Not glow. Just tactile.

The whole thing should read as: this belongs to a group of people, and they’ve been here.
